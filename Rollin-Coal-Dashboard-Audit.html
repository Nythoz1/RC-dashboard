1 · Executive Summary

Overall grade today: B− as a management tool · D as a place where wins feel good. The bones are genuinely strong: a serialized engine passport model, a reman lifecycle board, channel-listing tracking, and (as of this week) reliable cloud persistence with photos. The two things holding it back: the financial numbers can't be trusted yet (every engine has $0 cost, so "profit" is really revenue; the P&L mixes all-time revenue with monthly costs), and most modules are still empty (no invoices, quotes, cores, warranties, or time entries yet) — the tool tracks your iron but not yet your money or your people's work. The gamification plan in §6 is designed to fix adoption and fun at the same time: the game mechanics literally pull the missing data in.

3 · What's Solid (keep building on this)

  • The engine passport concept. Serialized units with lifecycle status, cost-basis breakdown, linked invoice/core/warranty/shipment records — this is the correct data model for a reman shop, and most off-the-shelf shop software gets it wrong.
  • Reman board. Drag/keyboard kanban with WIP capital in the header. This is your shop-floor heartbeat screen.
  • True-margin design intent. costBasis() summing core+freight+parts+labor is exactly how engine profit should be computed — it just needs data.
  • This week's fixes held under fire: photos persist (Storage bucket + instant attach), engines no longer vanish on edit, saves fail loudly, engines add by status (no qty), sold engines hit the P&L, thumbnails 2×.
  • Print styling for reports/inventory sheets, undo-able deletes, dark industrial brand consistently applied.

5 · Heavy-Duty Shop Lens — functional gaps that matter for a reman business

GapWhy a diesel shop needs it
Work orders don't link to enginesThe killer feature you're one join away from: tie a reman job to a unit, and every logged tech hour automatically feeds that engine's Machine + Assembly cost. True margin then computes itself instead of relying on someone typing labor cost by hand. This is the single highest-leverage build in the app.
Core bank agingYou buy and take cores; the cores module tracks deposits and due dates but sits empty. An aging view ("core out 45 days, $2,500 deposit exposed") protects real money.
Warranty auto-startSelling an engine should create the warranty with the clock started (sale date — see finding #2) instead of relying on someone remembering.
PO receiving → parts stockMarking a PO received should increment the parts quantities it contains; right now receiving is a status change only.
Customer units (VIN / unit #)Fleet customers think in unit numbers. Vehicles are free text today; structured units unlock "full history on unit 402" — a fleet-account selling point.
Test evidence on the passportCompression / blow-by numbers, oil pressure, a runner-video link. Doubles as listing ammo — "can be seen running" with proof attached.
AR aging (30/60/90)Once invoicing is live, overdue buckets drive collections. Blocked by finding #9 (real dates).

6 · Gamification Blueprint — "Make wins land"

Design rules first: one big moment, everything else subtle (if everything celebrates, nothing does); shop-floor aesthetic — stamps, plaques, flames, air-horns — never candy-crush; sounds always toggleable; celebrate the person as well as the number. Everything below runs on data the app already has or that findings #1–2 unlock.

#FeatureHow it worksWhat it drives
1The SOLD Moment flagshipFlipping an engine to sold slams a full-screen orange SOLD stamp over its passport, reveals the margin ("C13 · $29,000 · +$10.5k · 36%"), optional air-horn, and auto-posts to a permanent Wins feed. Undo-friendly (it's just the existing status change underneath).Makes the single most important event in the business feel like scoring. Also nudges cost entry — the margin reveal shows "?" if cost is $0.
2Monthly Goal ThermometerYou set a target (e.g. $60k or 4 engines/mo). A big gauge on Overview fills orange → overflows green past 100%. Beating your best month triggers a "🏆 BEST MONTH EVER" banner that stays up all day.Turns the Overview from a status page into a scoreboard. Needs sale dates (finding #2).
3Records Board (trophy case)Permanent plaques on Reports: Biggest Sale · Best Margin % · Fastest Reman (core→available) · Most Units in a Month — each with the engine, the number, and the date. Breaking one gets its own stamp moment.Memorable wins with names on them. Records persist — that's what makes them worth chasing.
4Reman Race TimersEvery board card shows days-in-stage. Beating the shop's average pace earns a small green flame; the longest resident gets called out in the column header. Finishing a reman (→ Available) gets a medium celebration + logs the turnaround time.WIP reduction becomes a race against your own average. Uses stage timestamps (finding #2).
5The Shift Card ("Daily 3")Each morning the app deals 3 tasks from real data: "2 available engines unlisted · 1 core deposit overdue · C13 has no cost basis." Punching all 3 stamps the day complete; consecutive days build a streak counter in the header.This is the adoption engine — it walks staff into the unused modules (invoicing, cores, listings) three taps at a time.
6Lot Health MetersFour completion gauges on Inventory: % photographed (12/14) · % listed · % with cost (0/14) · % with ESN (6/14). 100% on all four = "FULL LOT" badge on the header.Your audit findings #1 and #12, converted into a collection game. Filling the meters literally fixes the data.
7Tech of the WeekEvery Monday the app crowns last week's top tech from logged hours/billable $ (the Labor by Technician table already computes this). Small plaque on Team + the Wins feed.Makes techs want to log time — which is exactly the data that makes labor cost and engine cost basis true (§5 row 1).
8Weekly Recap CardFriday: a printable one-pager — "This week: 2 sold · $41k · 38h logged · 3 cores returned · streak 9 days." Same print styling as your inventory sheet; pin it in the break room.The fridge-door artifact. Wins that get printed get remembered.
Build order for fun: #1 SOLD Moment + Wins feed first (one afternoon, biggest emotional payoff), then #2 Goal Thermometer + #6 Lot Health Meters (they need only sale-date stamping and existing data), then #4/#5 once stage timestamps exist. #3, #7, #8 layer on cleanly after.
